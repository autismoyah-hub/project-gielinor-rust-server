# A repaired works, rather than decorated empty rooms

The narrative is that the workers restored a small common court and the essential water service before reclaiming the inner foundry. Repair is visible through a consistent family of dressed masonry, capped piers, fitted timber benches and service clusters. Age is concentrated in one outer repair scar. It is not spread as arbitrary rubble across every path.

## Architecture and scale

The entrance uses two successive pier pairs and a narrower vestibule before the sanctuary opens. The sanctuary's stepped outline creates service pockets; its single hearth and inward seating establish a social centre. The north prayer alcove and west bank counter share the same structural rhythm as the workshop's working bays. The wall/pier combination is repeated at doorways and the wheel foundation for a reason, rather than repeated as isolated decorative columns.

The workshop puts its largest mass at the water-side service wall. Small equipment is grouped as a bench line, shaping stations and fuel/delivery storage. This preserves the route from sanctuary to quay while giving the furnace a recognizable working context. The smaller basin replaces the first study's long, lightly dressed two-bank district: the effort belongs to four connected spaces, not another full-floor blockout.

The main route goes from the sanctuary through the workshop to the wheel; an alternate return goes through the quiet repair walk. The forward turn ends at a framed foundry firebox. No stairs, lower water passage or working hydraulic simulation is implied. The basin is 8×8 real water tiles; dry source chunks form the northern crossing. It is not a bridge over a rendered underpass.

## Terrain and materials

Use the existing textured grey paving for the refuge, entrance and quay, and a darker charcoal source for the southern shaping strip. The furnace and finishing benches sit on a fitted stone service apron rather than a large flat dark patch. The return walk keeps the paving: an earlier grass chunk bled green into the workshop through native underlay blending and read as a blockout patch. Ferns now mark damp joints around a small stone collapse instead. All terrain remains authentic copied chunks; there is no image retouching, synthetic floor painting or edited protected cache.

The native height difference between dry stone and water creates sloping bank/end seams. Boundaries protect the walking side, but the seams remain visible in the renders. This is a known artistic constraint of the current source selection, not a completed bespoke retaining-wall or sluice kit. Water-source research found no directly suitable intermediate-height chunk in the inspected local set. A different masonry/terrain solution may be needed if this remains below the desired bar.

## Close-view findings and revisions

The first broad study left too much canal-side extent and used a rubble definition whose native model looked like a black wedge. The refined sequence removes that extent and replaces the asset with a low stone pile. Log-like seats were replaced with fitted benches; paired seats now give the hearth a defined social space. The brighter inherited bank counter was compared with a restrained Keldagrim counter and a bank chest; the restrained counter supports the material family better.

The workshop arrival camera was turned toward the work instead of back into an empty aisle. A camera target initially landed in the furnace footprint; it moved to a genuinely clear tile, and every close-view stance is now included in the static audit. The wheel is inspected from multiple angles rather than presented only from the angle that hides its foundation or crops its face.

The optional authored object mode invokes the unchanged native library's static lighting/terrain-shadow path. Matched runtime/authored comparisons show better-grounded piers and more continuous wall lighting.
